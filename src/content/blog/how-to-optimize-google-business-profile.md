Every field in your Google Business Profile is a ranking signal. Most businesses fill in the bare minimum and leave most of their visibility on the table. We see this on nearly every profile we audit: the fields that take ten minutes to fix are usually the ones nobody has touched. A fully optimized GBP can outperform a neglected one even when the neglected profile has more reviews. Here's what to fix, in order of impact.

## The optimization hierarchy

1. Primary category, which determines which searches you qualify for at all
2. Business name, exact legal name only
3. NAP consistency across the web
4. Reviews: count, rating, recency, and response rate
5. Service list, where each service is its own relevance signal
6. Photos: volume, freshness, and real over stock
7. Business description, your full 750 characters
8. GBP posts, your weekly activity signal
9. Attributes: accessibility, ownership, language, and other qualifiers
10. Q&A section

## Primary category: the most important field

Your primary category decides which searches your profile even qualifies to appear in, not just where you rank within them. The rule is simple: pick the most specific match for your most important service. "Contractor" should be "Roofing Contractor." "Lawyer" should be "Personal Injury Attorney." Start typing your service into the GBP category field and use Google's exact suggested category, not something you invent. Add 3 to 5 relevant secondary categories, but don't pad with unrelated ones hoping to game the system. Google checks for that.

## Business name: don't get suspended

Your GBP name has to match your legal business name exactly, what's on your signage, your invoices, your license. "Garza Plumbing McAllen TX Emergency Plumber" isn't a stronger name than "Garza Plumbing." Adding city names or keywords doesn't help your ranking. It flags your profile for a terms violation, and the consequences range from lost ranking to a full suspension that can take weeks to appeal.

## Services list: each one is a search query

Most businesses list their primary category and stop there, which means they only show up for the single broadest search term. List every specific service you actually offer. An HVAC contractor shouldn't just list "HVAC service." List AC repair, AC installation, heat pump repair, mini-split installation, ductwork, duct cleaning, commercial HVAC, thermostat installation, and emergency service separately. A profile with 15 services is competing for 15 different searches, including the long-tail ones that tend to convert better.

## Reviews: the ranking multiplier

Google weighs recency first, then count, then rating, then how often you respond. Fifteen recent reviews can outrank sixty old ones. Build a simple collection habit: ask immediately after finishing a job, before you leave the property, then text the direct review link the same day. A QR code on your invoice works too. Aim for steady accumulation, a handful of new reviews each month, rather than a single burst. A sudden spike can actually trigger Google's spam filters.

Respond to every review, good or bad. For a negative one: thank them for the feedback, acknowledge their experience without admitting to anything inaccurate, and offer a direct way to resolve it. Keep it short and calm. You're not writing for the unhappy reviewer. You're writing for the next person who reads it.

## Photos: freshness beats perfection

Upload at least one new photo a week. Real photos consistently outperform stock images, and geotagging them to the city where the work happened helps with proximity relevance. Team photos build trust, before-and-after shots demonstrate results, and a branded vehicle at a job site signals you're an active, real business.

## Business description: use your 750 characters

A simple structure works well: open with what you do and your primary city, follow with the specific services you offer, add what makes you different (years in business, family-owned, bilingual), then close with your full service area. For example: "Rodriguez HVAC is a family-owned HVAC contractor serving McAllen, Edinburg, and Mission. We handle AC repair, installation, heat pump service, mini-splits, and indoor air quality for residential and commercial customers. With licensed technicians and same-day emergency service, we serve the entire Valley from our McAllen location."

## Attributes: the hidden signals

Ownership (women-owned, veteran-owned, Latino-owned), language (Spanish-speaking, bilingual), service options, accessibility, and accepted payments all matter for voice search and qualified "near me" queries, like someone searching for an HVAC company near them that speaks Spanish.

## GBP posts: your weekly activity signal

Posts expire after seven days. That's intentional. Google wants to see you posting regularly, and it takes about 10 minutes a week: a before-and-after from a recent job, a service spotlight, a seasonal reminder, or a customer testimonial with permission. Active profiles tend to get featured more prominently in branded search results, and a recent post is a simple signal that the business is currently operating.

## Q&A section: an underused keyword opportunity

The Q&A section is searchable and indexed. Seed it yourself with the questions customers actually ask: do you offer emergency service, what areas do you serve, are you licensed and insured, do you offer free estimates. Answer each one fully. These show up in search results and pre-sell the customer before they ever click through.

## Frequently asked questions

### How often should I update my GBP?

Weekly: one post, one new photo, and a response to any new review.

### Can I change my primary category later?

Yes, but it may temporarily affect your rankings while Google reprocesses the profile.

### Does posting on GBP actually help ranking?

It signals an active, operating business. Consistency matters more than the content of any individual post.

### Should I use stock photos?

No. Real photos of your team, your work, and your location consistently outperform stock.

### What's the single highest-impact optimization?

Primary category. It determines whether you qualify for relevant searches at all, before anything else even matters.
