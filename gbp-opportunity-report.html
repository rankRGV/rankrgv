Your Action Plan
The 3 things that will move your ranking
1
Get your first 10 Google reviews — this week
Google's algorithm treats how many reviews you've gotten in the last 90 days as the biggest indicator of whether your business is active and trustworthy. Reviews from 2 years ago barely count. Recent reviews count heavily.

The #1 business in your market has 8 reviews. You only need to match that to become competitive. The right way to do this: personal phone calls to people you've worked with, helped, or know. Not texts. Not emails. A 60-second call — "I'm trying to build our Google presence, would you leave us a quick review?" — converts 40–60% of the time. A mass text converts under 5%.

One call a day for two weeks. That's how you reach 10. Then keep going — 2 per week after that to maintain momentum.
Highest Impact
2
Complete the profile — it's missing critical information
Your Google Business Profile description is partially filled out and your photo count is very low. Google uses both of these to decide how relevant your listing is to a searcher.

Description: Google gives you 750 characters to describe your business. You're using a fraction of that. A complete description — one that mentions your services, your cities, and what makes you different — tells Google exactly who to show your profile to. Your updated description is ready to paste in.

Photos: Profiles with more photos get more clicks. More clicks tell Google the listing is valuable, which improves your ranking. Upload 5–10 real photos this week — your team, your work, your process. Then add one new photo every week going forward. This is a 30-minute task with a compounding return.

Q&A section: Anyone can post questions on your profile — and answer them, whether they know your business or not. You should seed 5 questions with accurate, keyword-rich answers before a stranger does it wrong.
High Impact · Fix Today
3
Respond to every review within 24 hours — including future ones
As reviews come in, responding to each one is both a trust signal to future customers and a freshness signal to Google. A profile that gets new reviews and responds to them looks active. An active profile ranks better.

Keep responses short and genuine. Mention what you helped them with and where they're located — "Thank you for trusting RankRGV with your local SEO in McAllen" — that's it. Don't write a press release. Just acknowledge them and move on.

If you ever get a negative review: don't argue, don't explain, don't get defensive. Offer to make it right offline — "We'd like to connect and fix this, please call us at 956-391-5991." That response matters more to future customers reading it than the negative review itself.
